Pricing & Planned Budget — key terms

How the four terms relate in this tool, from first estimate to the reference you track against.

Estimated budget→ Price proposal→ Planned budget→ Budget baseline

Estimated budget

The bottom-up cost estimate: for every WBS line, quantity × unit cost. It is your honest expected cost of doing the work, before any risk allowance, contingency or profit is added.

In the tool: Base Cost (Pricing summary). Now: —

Price proposal

The price you offer the client. It starts from the estimate, applies a risk factor to each line, then adds a contingency reserve and your profit margin on top.

In the tool: Total Tender Price = risk-adjusted cost + contingency + profit. Now: — (risk-adjusted —, contingency —, profit —)

Planned budget

The cost you plan to spend, line by line, to deliver the project. It excludes your profit, and the contingency reserve is held separately instead of being spread over the lines. Each line can also carry planned start and end dates.

In the tool: the Plan Total of each WBS line on the Budget tab (risk-adjusted cost). Now: —

Budget baseline

The agreed version of the planned budget (and schedule) that you freeze at approval and measure actual cost and progress against, so variance always means “versus what we agreed”.

In the tool: the planned budget and planned dates are what Actuals are compared to on the Budget tab, the Schedule and the Dashboard. The tool does not lock a snapshot automatically, so use Save Project File As… when the budget is approved to keep a frozen copy of the baseline.

Tip: changes to line items on this tab update the planned budget everywhere, so treat the saved project file as your approved baseline.

Recommended schedule

Proposes planned start and end dates for every task. Task length comes from quantity and unit (8 hours = 1 day, 1 month = 21 working days; other units default to 5 days). Sections and sub-sections run one after another, tasks inside a section run in parallel, and your dependencies are always respected. Nothing changes until you press Apply, and you can undo it afterwards.
